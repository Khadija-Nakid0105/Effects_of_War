# 📈 Geopolitical Risk & Capital Flight: The "War Premium" Framework

An end-to-end quantitative analytics framework engineered to isolate, model, and mathematically measure the "War Premium" fear factor on global safe-haven assets during geopolitical crises. 

---

## 🔬 Core Analytical Methodology & Tech Stack
* **Data Core & Query Layer:** DuckDB (High-performance relational aggregation on complex financial timelines)
* **Statistical Modeling Engine:** Ridge Regression (L2 Regularization to counter extreme multi-collinearity during market panics)
* **Feature Engineering:** Time-Series Lags, Rolling Volatility Windows, and Day-0 Geopolitical Shock Alignment

---

## 🚀 Key Data Engineering & Modeling Architecture

### 1. High-Performance Financial Aggregation (DuckDB)
* Leveraged **DuckDB** to execute ultra-fast data manipulations, temporal alignments, and multi-asset merges across disparate historical market layers without memory overhead.

### 2. Multi-Collinearity Mitigation via L2 Regularization
* Implemented **Ridge Regression** to isolate true geopolitical anomalies from standard market momentum. By penalizing the size of the regression coefficients ($L2$ regularization), the model stabilizes variable weights even when macroeconomic indicators shift simultaneously during international conflicts.

### 3. Quantitative Feature Engineering
* Constructed advanced mathematical indicators including custom time-series lags and dynamic rolling standard deviation bands to capture macro market velocity before and after crisis breakout thresholds.

---

## 📊 Strategic Business & Market Insights

> 🔍 **The Volatility Phenomenon:** The framework successfully isolates the exact mathematical breakout point where traditional macroeconomic realities (like inflation and interest rates) lose explanatory power, and emotional "War Panic" parameters temporarily dictate global capital allocation. It maps the precise velocity at which global capital flees high-risk equities to seek shelter in traditional safe-haven assets (Gold/USD).
